# Model notes

How the HUNTeR replica is built, what each simplification costs, and where the numbers come from. Values quoted here are generated by `tools/vehicle_design.py`; the authoritative list, with the status and source of every parameter, is [`DESIGN_REPORT.md`](DESIGN_REPORT.md).

## Frames and kinematic tree

```
odom (wheel odometry, four_ws_controller)
└─ base_footprint                    URDF root: ground projection of base_link at kerb static height
   └─ base_link (fixed)              hull, sprung mass 2394 kg; mid-wheelbase, wheel-centre height, x forward, z up
      ├─ front_axle_heave_joint      prismatic z, -0.092 .. +0.158 m (droop .. bump), spring + damper
      │  └─ front_axle_roll_joint    revolute x through the Panhard rod axis, +/-7.94 deg, spring + damper
      │     └─ front_axle_link       beam, differential, traction motor, steering actuator (285 kg)
      │        ├─ front_left_steer_joint     revolute z at the kingpin, +/-35 deg, position controlled
      │        │  └─ front_left_knuckle_link    knuckle, hub, rotor, caliper, high-steer arm (32 kg)
      │        │     └─ front_left_wheel_joint  continuous y, velocity controlled
      │        │        └─ front_left_wheel_link   tyre + beadlock rim (102 kg)
      │        └─ front_right_...
      ├─ rear_axle_... (droop 0.095 / bump 0.155 m; the rear axle is a front axle turned by 180 deg)
      └─ sensors (fixed): front/rear_lidar, cameras + optical frames, gnss, imu
```

Every camera has a `<name>_optical_frame` (z forward) used as the `frame_id` of its images. Lidar points are in `<name>_sensor_frame` (optical centre). The odometry publishes `odom -> base_footprint`, so the tree stays single-parented (REP 105).

## Suspension

### Real system (published)

Dependent suspension, rigid beam axles, "3-link type with Panhard bar, coil springs, controlled damping shock absorbers", stroke 25 cm (PIAP INTARG 2021 poster, via search summaries); "three reaction rods and a Panhard rod with screw springs and adjustable shock absorbers" (Defence24 2023).

### Replica design

The linkage hard points (`suspension.linkage` in `vehicle.yaml`) are a design, laid out to clear the 1 m tyres at full lock and full bump and to clear the axle-mounted parts:

| Element | Length | Placement |
|---|---|---|
| Lower trailing links (2) | 0.91 m | under the tubes, 0.42 m either side of the centre |
| Upper link (1) | 0.86 m | on a tower 0.25 m to the right of the centre: clear of the traction motor and above the high-steer tie rod over the whole stroke |
| Panhard rod | 1.00 m | 0.10 m above the axle centreline, 0.22 m outboard of the axle (ahead of the differential cover) |
| Coil-overs (2) | 0.64 m eye to eye | on the tubes, 1.24 m apart; 0.40 m body, 0.45 m rod |

The near-parallel lower and upper links put the side-view instant centre 44 m away, so the axle pitches less than 0.3 deg over the stroke. The Panhard rod arc shifts the axle 12.5 mm sideways at full bump.

Springs are sized **per axle** for the same ride frequency, so both ends have nearly the same static sag and each spring just unloads at its own full droop:

| Step | Formula | Front | Rear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sprung mass per corner | static sprung load / g / 2 | 660 kg | 537 kg |
| Ride rate for 1.5 Hz | m (2 pi f)^2 | 58.7 kN/m | 47.7 kN/m |
| Wheel rate (tyre 350 kN/m in series) | K_r k_t / (k_t - K_r) | 70.5 kN/m | 55.2 kN/m |
| Damping, zeta 0.35 | 2 zeta sqrt(k_w m) | 4.78 kN s/m | 3.81 kN s/m |
| Static sag = droop travel | W / k_w | 92 mm | 95 mm |
| Bump travel | 250 mm - droop | 158 mm | 155 mm |
| Roll stiffness, incl. tyres | (2 k_w (s/2)^2 + k_arb) in series with 2 k_t (T/2)^2, s = 1.24 m, k_arb = 15 kN m/rad | 61.7 kN m/rad | 52.2 kN m/rad |

Roll gradient: m_s g (h_s - h_rc) / (K_phi - m_s g (h_s - h_rc)) = 5.0 deg/g with the sprung CG at 0.98 m and the roll centre at 0.59 m. Wheel-hop frequency 6.2 Hz: the unsprung mass is high (553 kg per axle) because the traction motors sit on the axles ("two steering axles, each integrated with an electric motor").

### URDF equivalent and its error

The 3-link + Panhard rod leaves the axle two degrees of freedom relative to the hull (heave and roll), which the URDF reproduces with a prismatic and a revolute joint. The simulated tyre is rigid (its radius is the 0.492 m rolling radius), so the joint springs carry the coil springs **in series with the tyres** (heave 117 / 95 kN/m per axle, roll 61.7 / 52.2 kN m/rad). The body therefore keeps the design 1.5 Hz ride frequency and the design static drop (spring sag + tyre deflection); the spring references put the joints at zero at kerb weight, so payload sags the suspension. What is neglected:

* axle pitch and fore-aft motion from the 3-link geometry (under 0.3 deg) and the 12.5 mm Panhard shift;
* per-wheel travel limits: the joint limits form a box in (heave, roll) space while the real limit is per wheel, so combined extreme heave + roll can exceed the stroke at one wheel by up to ~0.12 m in corner cases;
* tyre dynamics: with a rigid tyre the wheel-hop mode and tyre enveloping are not represented.

The physical links are drawn in RViz by `suspension_linkage_publisher` between their chassis and axle hard points; because the axle pitch and lateral shift are not solved, the drawn rigid links change length by a few centimetres over the full travel.

## Steering

### Real system (published / assumed)

Both axles steer (published). PIAP asked for Ultimate Dana 60 (or equivalent) rigid front axles, three of them, in tender ZO/11/07/2017. The winning offer could not be read, so the replica assumes Dana 60-type steer axles at both ends, steering through knuckle arms and a tie rod, with one actuator per axle (actuator type not published).

### Replica

* Steering axis: vertical through the kingpin centre (the Dana 60 kingpin inclination of ~8 deg and caster of ~5 deg are documented but not modelled; the camber change they would add while steering reaches about 4 deg at full lock).
* Kingpin track 1.538 m (70 in wheel-mount width minus 2 x 0.12 m), spindle offset 0.133 m to the tyre centre plane.
* High-steer arms: the tie rod runs 0.21 m above the axle centreline, over the differential and pinion nose (15 mm clearance), with a linear actuator on the axle beside it.
* Steering trapezoid: arm 0.155 m, inclined 38.2 deg, optimised for counter-phase 4WS (instantaneous centre on the mid-wheelbase line), RMS Ackermann error 0.35 deg over the lock range. Arms laid out for a 2.946 m two-wheel-steer wheelbase (the Jeep JK Unlimited the crate axle is sold for) would give 4.3 deg RMS error in 4WS, so the arms are assumed to be modified.
* Each knuckle is its own URDF joint. The controller computes linkage-consistent left/right pairs: in turns the pair whose Ackermann-equivalent centre angle matches the commanded geometry, in crab the pair whose mean angle matches the commanded direction. While steering it slews the left knuckle and derives the right one from the four-bar, as a single actuator and tie rod would. `linkage:=ideal` replaces this with exact per-wheel Ackermann angles.

Consequences you will see in simulation: tiny tyre scrub in 2WS (the trapezoid over-corrects for front-only steering, which also enlarges the 2WS turning radius from 6.16 to 7.00 m), and a ~1 deg track-angle error in crab mode.

## Drive controller

`four_ws_controller` shapes commands before they reach ros2_control:

* the path geometry (curvature for the ICR modes, track angle for crab) is applied at once and only the speed is ramped, so the vehicle follows the commanded arc while it accelerates;
* speed is capped by the steering limits, the yaw-rate and lateral-acceleration limits (rollover protection) and by how far the knuckles still lag their target, all through the same acceleration/deceleration ramp;
* the steering reference never exceeds what the steering can realise (no windup); a rotate-in-place request pre-steers to full lock without moving;
* wheel odometry is a least-squares fit using one no-side-slip constraint per axle (at the Ackermann-equivalent angle) and one rolling constraint per wheel (one per axle with locked differentials); it inverts the controller exactly in every ICR mode, linkage and differential setting.

## Drive train

Two PMSM traction motors (PIAP notice ZWR/01/2018: up to 700 VDC, at least 4500 rpm at full load), one on each axle's pinion, 90 kW continuous / 220 kW peak in total. A reduction of 2.98:1 ahead of the 5.38 ring-and-pinion is estimated so the motors reach 4500 rpm at 52 km/h. In the simulation each wheel is velocity controlled with a 4300 N m torque limit (peak motor torque through the gearing, split by the differential); the controller's `electronic` differential mode commands kinematically consistent wheel speeds and `locked` equal speeds per axle.

## Hull

The hull is the closed, faceted body of the 2023-2025 vehicle, measured off press photos against the 1 m wheel (status `photo` in the design report). It is assembled from convex plate bodies in `tools/generate_meshes.py`: the full-width upper hull over the wheels (arch tops 1.20 m, deck 1.46 m, side flanks leaning ~20 deg inward), the nose block with the ~41 deg glacis, the black sensor band (0.80-1.07 m, flat centre, ends wrapped back to the corners with two LED lamps each), the chin whose lower plates converge on the front tow bar, the tub between the wheels (trapezoidal in side view, sides leaning inward), and the rear: a dark anthracite section with a gently sloping rear deck and a steep rear face above the olive rear panel, open underneath. Where no photo shows a detail squarely, the replica's clearances decide: the nose and tail blocks start ahead of the steered tyre sweep (1.97 m), the arch tops sit above the tyre at full bump, and the inclined tub ends stay clear of the axle motors. `tools/check_clearance.py` verifies the hull meshes against tyres, wheels, axles and knuckles over the steering lock and the per-wheel suspension travel.

## Mass and inertia

The 3500 kg kerb mass is split into a documented budget (kept from the 2020 open-deck build when the hull was reshaped, so the dynamics validation stays valid; the closed hull would raise the CG by roughly 2 cm): genset 360 kg, battery 300 kg, power electronics, cooling, fuel, body, frame as the remainder. The sensors are separate URDF links (about 4 kg) and are taken out of the budget, so the model totals 3504 kg. The chassis inertia tensor is the parallel-axis sum of the budget blocks: Ixx 700, Iyy 3930, Izz 4421 kg m^2 about the sprung COM at 0.98 m height, 0.14 m ahead of mid-wheelbase. The axle link inertia is likewise a parallel-axis sum (housing, motor, actuator) about its own COM.

## Collision model

Hull (16 boxes, two of them inclined): upper hull, glacis plate, sensor band, chin, tub (three tiers following its trapezoidal sides), keel/tunnels (above the highest axle-mounted part at full bump), rear upper hull, rear deck and rear face plates, rear panel and the two tow bars, matching the visual hull. The lowest points are the tow bars (0.40 m front, 0.50 m rear). Axles: a sphere at each differential (the lowest point, 0.327 m ground clearance). Tyres: cylinders, unnamed so the tyre friction (mu 0.9) reaches Gazebo. Gazebo uses DART with the Bullet collision detector at a 1 ms step.
