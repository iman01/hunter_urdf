# Model notes

How the HUNTeR replica is built, what each simplification costs, and where the numbers come from. Values quoted here are generated by `tools/vehicle_design.py`; the authoritative list is [`DESIGN_REPORT.md`](DESIGN_REPORT.md).

## Frames and kinematic tree

```
base_footprint                       ground projection of base_link at kerb static height
└─ base_link (fixed)                 hull, sprung mass 2394 kg; mid-wheelbase, wheel-centre height, x forward, z up
   ├─ front_axle_heave_joint         prismatic z, -0.104 .. +0.146 m (droop .. bump), spring + damper
   │  └─ front_axle_roll_joint       revolute x through the Panhard rod axis, +/-7.94 deg, spring + damper
   │     └─ front_axle_link          beam, differential, traction motor, steering actuator (285 kg)
   │        ├─ front_left_steer_joint    revolute z at the kingpin, +/-35 deg, position controlled
   │        │  └─ front_left_knuckle_link   knuckle, hub, rotor, caliper (32 kg)
   │        │     └─ front_left_wheel_joint continuous y, velocity controlled
   │        │        └─ front_left_wheel_link  tyre + beadlock rim (102 kg)
   │        └─ front_right_...
   ├─ rear_axle_... (same; the rear axle is a front axle turned by 180 deg)
   ├─ sensors (fixed): front/rear_lidar, cameras + optical frames, gnss, imu, PTZ (pan/tilt joints)
   └─ payload: cargo | rws (yaw/pitch joints) | uav_pad, variant_2023_mass
```

Every camera has a `<name>_optical_frame` (z forward) used as the `frame_id` of its images. Lidar points are in `<name>_sensor_frame` (optical centre).

## Suspension

### Real system (published)

Dependent suspension, rigid beam axles, "3-link type with Panhard bar, coil springs, controlled damping shock absorbers", stroke 25 cm (PIAP INTARG 2021 poster); "three reaction rods and a Panhard rod with screw springs and adjustable shock absorbers" (Defence24 2023).

### Replica design

The linkage hard points (`suspension.linkage` in `vehicle.yaml`) are a design: two lower trailing links (0.91 m), one upper link (0.71 m) offset over the differential, a 1.17 m Panhard rod 0.10 m above the axle centreline, and coil-overs on the axle tubes 1.24 m apart. They were placed to clear the 1 m tyres at full lock and full bump.

Spring and damper sizing (per corner, kerb mass):

| Step | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Sprung mass per corner | (3500 kg - 1106 kg unsprung) / 4 | 598.5 kg |
| Ride rate for 1.5 Hz | m (2 pi f)^2 | 53.2 kN/m |
| Wheel rate (tyre 350 kN/m in series) | K_r k_t / (k_t - K_r) | 62.7 kN/m |
| Damping, zeta 0.35 | 2 zeta sqrt(k_w m) | 4.29 kN s/m |
| Static sag (front) | W / k_w | 104 mm => droop travel |
| Bump travel | 250 - 104 | 146 mm |
| Roll stiffness per axle | 2 k_w (s/2)^2 + k_arb, s = 1.24 m, k_arb = 15 kN m/rad | 63.2 kN m/rad |
| Roll gradient | m_s g (h_s - h_rc) / (K_phi - m_s g (h_s - h_rc)) | 4.6 deg/g |
| Wheel-hop frequency | sqrt(2 (k_w + k_t) / m_unsprung) / 2 pi | 6.1 Hz |

The unsprung mass is high (553 kg per axle) because the traction motors sit on the axles ("two steering axles, each integrated with an electric motor").

### URDF equivalent and its error

The 3-link + Panhard rod leaves the axle two degrees of freedom relative to the hull (heave and roll), which the URDF reproduces exactly with a prismatic and a revolute joint. What is neglected:

* axle pitch from the 3-link geometry: the axle rotates about the side-view instant centre 8.3 m away, about 1 deg at full bump (caster change);
* Panhard rod arc: lateral axle shift of 9 mm at full bump;
* per-wheel travel limits: the joint limits form a box in (heave, roll) space while the real limit is per wheel, so combined extreme heave + roll can exceed the stroke at one wheel by up to ~0.12 m in corner cases;
* tyre compliance: the simulated tyre is rigid (its radius equals the 0.492 m rolling radius), so wheel hop is stiffer than in reality and the body rides 16 mm higher than on a deflected tyre.

The coil-over springs are linear down to full droop (no helper spring), matching the design choice that the spring just unloads at full droop.

## Steering

### Real system (published / estimated)

Both axles steer (published). PIAP bought Ultimate Dana 60 front axles for both ends (tender ZO/11/07/2017), which steer through knuckle arms and a tie rod; one electric actuator per axle is the most likely arrangement (estimated).

### Replica

* Steering axis: vertical through the kingpin centre (the Dana 60 kingpin inclination of ~8 deg and caster of ~5 deg are documented but not modelled; the camber change they would add while steering reaches about 4 deg at full lock).
* Kingpin track 1.538 m (70 in wheel-mount width minus 2 x 0.12 m), spindle offset 0.133 m to the tyre centre plane.
* Steering trapezoid: arm 0.155 m, inclined 38.2 deg, optimised for counter-phase 4WS (instantaneous centre on the mid-wheelbase line), RMS Ackermann error 0.35 deg over the lock range. The stock Jeep JK geometry of the crate axle would give 4.3 deg RMS error in 4WS, so the arms are assumed to be modified.
* Each knuckle is its own URDF joint. The controller computes the linkage-consistent left/right pair: in turns the pair whose Ackermann-equivalent centre angle matches the commanded geometry, in crab the pair whose mean angle matches the commanded direction. `linkage:=ideal` replaces this with exact per-wheel Ackermann angles.

Consequences you will see in simulation: tiny tyre scrub in 2WS (the trapezoid over-corrects for front-only steering, which also enlarges the 2WS turning radius from 6.16 to 7.00 m), and a ~1.5 deg track-angle error in crab mode.

## Drive train

Two PMSM traction motors (PIAP notice ZWR/01/2018: up to 700 VDC, at least 4500 rpm at full load), one on each axle's pinion, 90 kW continuous / 220 kW peak in total. A reduction of 2.98:1 ahead of the 5.38 ring-and-pinion is estimated so the motors reach 4500 rpm at 52 km/h. In the simulation each wheel is velocity controlled with a 4300 N m torque limit (peak motor torque through the gearing, split by the open differential); the controller's `electronic` differential mode commands kinematically consistent wheel speeds and `locked` equal speeds per axle.

## Mass and inertia

The 3500 kg kerb mass is split into a documented budget (genset 360 kg, battery 300 kg, power electronics, cooling, fuel, body, frame as the remainder). The chassis inertia tensor is the parallel-axis sum of these blocks: Ixx 708, Iyy 3947, Izz 4431 kg m^2 about the sprung COM at 1.01 m height, 0.15 m ahead of mid-wheelbase. The armed variant adds the 205 kg ZMU-03 (published) and 345 kg of growth to reach the published "over 4 t".

## Collision model

Hull: five boxes (deck/fenders, centre tub, keel/tunnels, nose, tail) matching the visual hull. Axles: a sphere at each differential (the lowest point, 0.327 m ground clearance). Tyres: cylinders. Gazebo uses DART with the Bullet collision detector at a 1 ms step.
